world labs

Long-horizon tasks in interactive worlds.

World Labs is building environments inside game engines to train and evaluate AI agents on long-horizon tasks.

We focus on two connected problems:

  • Designing tasks that span planning, building, testing, and debugging
  • Verifying that agents finish the job without breaking earlier work

Starting with Roblox Studio, agents work across code, 3D scenes, and live gameplay to build functioning games. Each decision changes the project; later steps depend on earlier work.

These tasks demand sustained execution: tracking state, testing assumptions, and recovering from errors. We’re developing verifiable outcomes and trajectories for reinforcement learning, post-training, and evaluation.

We’re also developing human-data projects across game engines and browser games, including WebGL experiences. Expert demonstrations, critiques, comparisons, and revisions capture the human judgment behind game development, design, and playtesting.

To discuss an environment pilot or a human-data project, get in touch at dhruv@tryworldlabs.com.

Interact Bench

Evaluating AI agents on building and debugging interactive games

October 2, 2026

We present Interact Bench, a benchmark of 117 tasks that evaluates AI agents on game development inside Roblox Studio. Across 87 code-generation tasks and 30 debugging tasks, agents modify scenes, implement game mechanics, and repair existing scripts. Tasks run in a game-engine environment, with automated checks that verify changes to the scene and behavior during gameplay.

We compare frontier models across both task suites, measuring first-attempt success, success within five attempts, consistency, and tool-call errors. The charts below show Pass@1; the full results include all five metrics for each model.

Worldline Bench

Under development

Evaluating AI agents across long-horizon tasks in interactive worlds

Worldline Bench is being designed around extended development assignments that require agents to turn an open-ended goal into a working game system. Agents will need to break down requirements, coordinate changes across code and scenes, and carry decisions through many dependent steps. A design choice made early may only reveal its consequences much later—when systems interact, playtests expose edge cases, or a new feature breaks existing behavior.

The challenge is carrying the whole assignment through: tracking unfinished work, revising a plan as evidence changes, recovering from failed approaches, and verifying that the final experience works as a coherent whole.

Worldline Bench is under development. The task suite and evaluation methodology are being developed; results have not yet been published.
