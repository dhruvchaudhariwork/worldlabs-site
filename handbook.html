The contributor handbook.

A guide to joining the community, finding a project, and turning your game expertise into useful data.

Where we are today

Applications are open and our first projects are in development. This guide describes our intended project process. There are no open assignments or contributor accounts yet; we’ll contact potential matches by email.

01 / After you apply

Your application is submitted to our private review inbox. The confirmation on the application page means we received it; it does not mean you have been approved or selected for a project.

We’ll review your credits, portfolio, specialties, and availability as projects take shape. If there’s a match, we’ll contact you using the email address you supplied. There is no guaranteed response date or assignment.

Need to correct your application or update your availability? Email us from the address you applied with. Please don’t send confidential material from past employers or clients.

02 / Finding the right project

Community participation and project selection are separate. Each project will have its own needs, which may include a particular genre, tool, platform, or design discipline.

What to expect in an invitation

  • The project objective, required expertise, and expected deliverables.
  • The time commitment, deadlines, compensation, and payment timing.
  • Any qualification or calibration task, with its terms provided before you begin.
  • Instructions for accepting or declining and a point of contact for questions.

A project may include a paid sample assignment to check fit and align on the review criteria. Selection will depend on the project’s requirements, relevant experience, availability, and qualification results.

03 / Contributing your craft

Assignments may include writing briefs, building a mechanic, playtesting, diagnosing bugs, evaluating outputs, or revising designs. A development-trajectory project would connect the original task to your implementation, observations, changes, and retest evidence. The brief will explain what to capture and how the work will be reviewed.

Make your reasoning useful

  • Judge against the intended genre, audience, and design goals.
  • Point to evidence: a play observation, artifact, calculation, or reproducible issue.
  • Explain tradeoffs. A tie or insufficient evidence may be more accurate than a forced preference.
  • For feel or interaction claims, use playable evidence. A screenshot alone cannot establish how a game controls.

Before starting an assignment

Project terms will define permitted use of your contributions, confidentiality, attribution, and any rules for AI tools. Use only materials you are permitted to contribute. Any recording of editor activity, code changes, or playtest events must be specified in the project terms before capture begins. Do not include private communications, unrelated work, or player data without the agreed permissions and consent. Ask about unclear requirements before submitting work.

Review and revisions

Each project will specify its acceptance criteria and revision expectations. Reviewers may ask for clearer evidence, missing information, or corrections. Keep the original artifacts and the reasoning behind your changes available for review.

04 / Compensation and timing

We intend to run paid projects and paid sample assignments. Rates, payment structure, acceptance criteria, and payment timing will be agreed for each project before you accept work.

There is no universal hourly rate or payment schedule at this stage. Applying does not guarantee work or payment. Any future project invitation should give you enough detail to decide whether the assignment works for you.

05 / Keep in touch

For questions about your application or the community, contact dhruv@tryworldlabs.com. If you are asking about an existing application, use the email address you applied with.
