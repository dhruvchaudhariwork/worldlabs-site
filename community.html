What happens after applying ↗

  1. Tell us what you know.

    Share your portfolio, specialties, engine experience, and availability through the expert application.

  2. We review for a match.

    We’ll review applications as projects take shape and contact you by email if there’s a fit. An application isn’t a project offer.

  3. Get familiar with the work.

    A project may include a paid sample or calibration task. You’ll receive the brief and terms before deciding to take part.

  4. Agree, then get started.

    Selected contributors agree on deliverables, rates, and timing, then receive the instructions for their assignment.

Game developer — create from a brief

Playable prototypes / demonstrations · Example assignment
+

Build a small playable experience from scratch within a defined scope. For example, create a short platformer with checkpoints, a moving platform, and a finish condition.

Example deliverable: An editable project, a playable build, and evidence that the required behaviors work. Depending on the assignment, include intermediate versions, a walkthrough of key steps, and explanations of design decisions.

Gameplay programmer — diagnose & repair

Engine behavior / multiplayer · Example assignment
+

Inspect an AI-generated implementation of a shared door that opens for one player but stays closed for another. Reproduce the failure, identify the cause, and correct the behavior.

Example deliverable: Before-and-after project versions, the changes you made, a diagnosis tied to observed behavior, and retests covering both players and previously working interactions.

QA specialist — test & evaluate

Behavior checks / reproducible evidence · Example assignment
+

Turn a task brief into repeatable checks, then evaluate an AI-created build. For a checkpoint task, verify where a player respawns before and after touching the checkpoint.

Example deliverable: The test setup, steps, expected and observed outcomes, and supporting logs or clips. Report requirement checks separately from judgments about clarity or player experience.

Combat designer — compare & critique

Combat / game feel · Example assignment
+

Compare two playable prototypes made for the same brief. Explain differences in telegraphing, recovery windows, feedback, and counterplay.

Example deliverable: A preference, criterion-level tradeoffs, and evidence from play. Ties and insufficient evidence are valid outcomes.

Systems designer — evaluate & revise

Economies / progression · Example assignment
+

Review a crafting or upgrade economy. Identify resource bottlenecks, dominant purchases, or unreachable recipes under the brief’s stated assumptions.

Example deliverable: An evidence-backed critique, corrected tables, and the reasoning behind your changes.

Level designer — build & explain

Levels / onboarding · Example assignment
+

Create or revise a puzzle level that introduces a mechanic through play. Explain how its layout teaches the player and preserves solvability.

Example deliverable: Versioned level artifacts, design rationale, playtest observations, and a record of revisions and retests.

Game specialist — write the brief

Across disciplines · Example assignment
+

Turn a design intention into a self-contained prompt. Specify the genre, audience, platform, constraints, and intended player experience.

Example deliverable: An expert-authored brief, context tags, required behaviors, and an explanation of the important choices.

These illustrate possible work, not available roles. Scope and requirements will be defined for each commissioned project.

The contributor handbook ↗
What happens after I apply?
Your application goes to our review team. We’ll reach out by email if your experience fits an upcoming project. For now, updates are handled by email; there is no contributor login or application-status dashboard.
Are there paid projects available now?
Our first projects are in development. We are gathering expert interest, and there are no open assignments yet. Future invitations will include the scope, compensation, and payment timing before you accept.
What experience are you looking for?
We’re looking for hands-on expertise in development, design, technical art, QA, or playtesting. Show us work you’ve built, designed, debugged, or tested, and explain your contribution and tools. Independent prototypes, studio projects, and specialist portfolios can all demonstrate relevant expertise. You do not need prior AI experience.
What would I submit?
It depends on the assignment: a playable project built from a brief, a corrected implementation, test results, a critique, or a comparison. Some projects will also ask for intermediate versions, recorded steps, and explanations of key decisions so the result can be understood in context. The brief will define the required files, evidence, and review criteria before you start.
How much time will I need?
Availability requirements will vary. Each project invitation will describe the expected time and deadlines so you can decide whether the work fits your schedule.
Does joining guarantee project work?
No. Project selection will depend on your specialty, availability, relevant work, and any project-specific qualification. There may be a wait before a suitable assignment becomes available.
How will my work be used?
Projects are intended to create data for AI training or evaluation. Each project will define permitted use, confidentiality, and attribution before work begins. Read more in the handbook.
